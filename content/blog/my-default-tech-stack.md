I have a confession: my stack is boring. Next.js, Tailwind, Vercel, markdown. I reach for the same tools on nearly every project, and I don't feel guilty about it.

## Why boring wins

Every tool you learn has a cost. When you switch frameworks on every project, you spend your energy on the *framework* instead of the *problem*. A boring stack means the tooling disappears and all your energy goes into the thing you actually want to build.

## The stack

- **Next.js** — pages, APIs and static sites in one place
- **Tailwind CSS** — styling without leaving my markup
- **Vercel** — deploy on every push, zero configuration
- **Markdown** — all content lives in files I can version

## When I break the rules

Boring doesn't mean rigid. I switch when there's a *real* reason:

- The problem genuinely needs a different tool
- A new tool is clearly and measurably better
- I'm learning something specific on purpose

Otherwise, I default back. This keeps a clear line between "deliberate choice" and "shiny-object distraction."

## The payoff

Because my defaults are stable, an idea goes from my head to a live URL in under an hour. That speed is the whole point. If I spend that hour fighting tooling instead, the idea often dies before it exists.

Choose boring where it counts. Spend the energy where it matters.
