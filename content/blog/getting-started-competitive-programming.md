Competitive programming can feel like a wall. Everyone online seems to have been solving 2000-rated problems since age ten. But the real entry path is much more accessible than the noise suggests.

## Pick one platform and stay there

Don't bounce between five platforms. Pick one (Codeforces, CodeChef, or LeetCode) and learn its conventions. You want to spend your energy on problem-solving, not on relearning input/output formats every week.

## Start with divisions, not ratings

Don't jump into rated contests. Start with the easier divisions and problems. The goal at first is not to rank — it's to build the muscle of *recognizing problem types*.

## The three core skills

Everything in competitive programming builds on three things:

1. **Reading** — understanding what the problem actually asks
2. **Recognizing** — matching it to a pattern you've seen
3. **Implementing** — turning the idea into correct code quickly

Most beginners are slow because they haven't practiced recognizing patterns. That's why I built Code Battle around timed, structured challenges — repetition is the fastest path.

## A sane weekly schedule

- **2–3 easy problems** to stay sharp
- **1–2 medium problems** to stretch
- **1 contest** per week, then *review the editorial afterward*

The review is the part everyone skips and the part that actually makes you better.

## The honest truth

You don't need to be a genius. You need to be consistent. Thirty minutes most days beats a four-hour binge once a month. Show up, and the improvement follows.
